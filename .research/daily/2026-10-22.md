# Research digest 2026-10-22

## New in AI and GenAI

- **Who judges the judge: 541,000 LLM-judge decisions audited** ([Kili Technology, Oct 16](https://kili-technology.com/blog/data-story-who-judges-the-judge-llm-as-a-judge)) — 21 judge models across MT-Bench, JudgeBench and RewardBench; raw agreement overstates judge quality by 33–41 points of Cohen's kappa on MT-Bench, and judge rankings don't transfer across benchmarks. Why it matters: if your eval pipeline reports raw agreement without chance correction, inter-annotator agreement, or order counterbalancing, this is the paper that tells you what to fix first — and it doubles as interview material.
- **DeepSeek open-sources DeepInfer** ([github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepInfer](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepInfer), Apache 2.0, ~Oct 21) — the serving stack behind DeepSeek's production traffic, supporting V4.1-Flash, with **batch-invariant inference and deterministic sampling** as first-class features. Why it matters: non-determinism from batching is a notorious source of flaky eval scores; this is a reference implementation of how to kill it.
- **MiniMax M3.1 Flash goes GA** ([VentureBeat, Oct 19](https://venturebeat.com/technology/minimaxs-new-m3-1-flash-beats-deepseek-and-opus-5-on-a-key-business-benchmark-at-lower-cost-but-with-a-high-hallucination-rate)) — $0.15/M input, $0.60/M output flat across a 1M context, strong agentic-benchmark numbers, but a flagged high hallucination rate; weights were promised for Oct 23. Why it matters: a textbook case of benchmark wins not implying deployment readiness — exactly the gap evaluation work exists to close.
- **Mercury 3, a diffusion LLM at scale** ([Inception Labs, Oct 19](https://www.inceptionlabs.ai/blog/introducing-mercury-3)) — parallel token generation at a vendor-measured 1,068 tokens/sec with a 1M context, claimed 4–8x faster than comparable autoregressive models. Why it matters: diffusion text models are moving from curiosity to a real latency/cost option, and they break assumptions baked into token-by-token eval harnesses.
- **Mistral Timestral, time-series foundation models** ([Mistral, Oct 15](https://mistral.ai/news/timestral)) — four sizes (5M–209M params), modified MIT license, probabilistic and multivariate forecasting with covariates, reported first place on GIFT-Eval, TIME and fev-bench. Why it matters: small, CPU-fast, permissively licensed foundation models are a reminder that not every ML job is an LLM job, and forecasting roles are plentiful in India.
- **Voxtral 3 for speech agents** ([Mistral, Oct 20](https://mistral.ai/news/voxtral-3)) — audio models aimed at agents that reason, speak and act. Why it matters: voice agents are one of the fastest-growing product categories hiring in India, and speech evaluation (WER plus task success) is an under-served specialism.

## System design and engineering

- **[The bridge to agent-scale Git infrastructure](https://github.blog/engineering/architecture-optimization/the-bridge-to-agent-scale-git-infrastructure/)** (GitHub, Oct 21) — GitHub has replicated all but 0.00001% of its repositories into Azure, and explains how per-reference quorums handle the remainder and how content-addressed, immutable Git objects let reads scale independently of writes. Read the companion piece, [Building Git infrastructure for agent-scale development](https://github.blog/engineering/architecture-optimization/building-git-infrastructure-for-agent-scale-development/) (Oct 6), for the driving numbers: pushes grew 4.9x year over year to 3.35 billion per month. This is a genuinely excellent worked example of separating storage from compute and of quorum design.
- **[From Microservices to Agents: the Next Evolution of Distributed Systems](https://www.infoq.com/articles/distributed-systems-microservices-agentic-ai/)** (InfoQ, Oct 19) — argues the hard part is shifting from service orchestration to decision orchestration, and that idempotency, backpressure and failure isolation still apply one abstraction layer up. Useful vocabulary for system-design rounds where the interviewer asks you to design an agentic system.
- **[Google Cloud infrastructure at NVIDIA GTC Berlin 2026](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/ai-infrastructure/google-cloud-infrastructure-at-nvidia-gtc-berlin-2026)** (Google Cloud, ~Oct 22) — the Virgo non-blocking scale-out mesh fabric, with clusters of 80,000 GPUs in one site and up to 960,000 multi-site. Worth skimming for the network-topology constraints that shape how large training and inference jobs are actually scheduled.

## Read today

**System design — Client-Server Model:** [MIT 6.005, Reading 21: Sockets & Networking](https://web.mit.edu/6.005/www/fa15/classes/21-sockets-networking/). Pick this over the blog-post explainers because it builds the model from the bottom up — listening socket versus connection socket, `accept` as the point where concurrency begins, buffering and the wire-protocol design that follows. It gives you the precise language for the stateless-versus-stateful and connection-handling questions that client-server interview rounds reliably reach.

**ML / GenAI — NumPy Fundamentals:** [Broadcasting, NumPy manual](https://numpy.org/doc/stable/user/basics.broadcasting.html). Broadcasting is the single concept that separates people who write loops from people who write array code: the right-to-left shape comparison rule, dimensions compatible when equal or 1, and `np.newaxis` for outer operations. Read the final section on when *not* to broadcast too — large intermediate arrays are a real memory trap when you are scoring a big eval set. Note that `np.vectorize` is a convenience wrapper around a Python loop, not a performance tool.

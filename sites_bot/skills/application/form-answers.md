# Form answers
Adapted from the job-application-assistant skill of ai-job-search-dev/ai-job-search (MIT).

Applies on every board and company site whenever a form asks for free text: a self-introduction,
"why this company", a project entry, a competency question, or a pitch under a character limit.

- Select, never add. Every claim must already be in the candidate facts or resume. The recruiter reads
  the form next to the resume, so a form field never introduces, inflates or rescopes anything.
- Interview test: the candidate must be able to explain each sentence in an interview without
  backtracking. Adjacent work is not described in the posting's exact terms; contributory work says
  so instead of reading as ownership. In-progress qualifications stay in progress.
- Lead with the strongest evidence and its number, not with chronology. A concrete situation beats an
  adjective: no "passionate", "fast learner", "team player", "hit the ground running", "great fit".
- End by tying the answer to this employer's actual work as shown on the job page. Generic text reads
  as generic.
- Introduction: current status, strongest evidence with scale, what they want next at this employer.
  Project entry: what it did and for whom, the hardest problem and how it was solved, the outcome.
  Project role and dates are the candidate's own on that project; never upgrade or invent them.
- Respect the limit shown on the field. Count characters from the page rather than estimating, since
  portals truncate silently mid-word. A chat box takes one sentence; a yes/no question takes Yes or No.
- Plain first person, one paragraph, no em or en dashes, exclamation marks, emoji, greeting or sign-off.
  Match the posting's language. If the facts cannot answer it, leave it and list it in `missing`.
